Camera-Noise Residuals for Face-Swap Detection:
Redundant, Not Complementary, and Why
Danil Davydov∗, Bader Rasheed†, Dmitriy Vatolin†
∗Innopolis University, Innopolis, Russia
†Laboratory of Innovative Technologies for Processing Video Content, Innopolis University, Innopolis, Russia
{d.davydov, b.rasheed, d.vatolin}@innopolis.university
Abstract—Fusing a learned camera-noise fingerprint with an
RGB appearance backbone is an appealing route to generator-
independent deepfake detection, because the noise residual is
grounded in image-formation physics rather than in the texture
statistics of a particular generator. We test, on FaceForensics++,
whether a Noiseprint++ residual channel carries information
complementary to an RGB Xception backbone for face-swap
detection. A three-model ablation (RGB-only, residual-only, late-
fusion) shows that fusion does not improve over RGB alone
and that the residual branch alone is near chance. A seven-
level bottleneck diagnostic localizes the cause: the noise maps
do carry a discriminative signal, but it is statistical—carried
by the per-sample first and second moments (mean, variance,
energy) of the residual—and the per-sample InstanceNorm
layer placed at the noise-branch input, following the TruFor
template, standardizes exactly those moments away (five-fold
cross-validated AUC drops from 0.747 to 0.554). A context-
crop control rules out cropping geometry, and two fixed-fusion
variants that remove the bottleneck recover the statistical signal
yet still fail to beat RGB on every dataset. We conclude that, on
this manipulation distribution, the noise residual is redundant
with RGB rather than complementary, and we give concrete
guidance for practitioners adopting noise-residual fusion for face-
swap detection.
Index Terms—deepfake detection, image forensics, sensor
noise, Noiseprint, face swapping, feature fusion, instance nor-
malization.
I. INTRODUCTION
Face-swap deepfakes have become photorealistic and cheap
to produce, eroding the reliability of visual media [1], [10].
Detectors trained on the texture-level artefacts of one generator
family often fail when confronted with a new architecture
or dataset, behaving as fingerprint matchers that memorize
dataset-specific quirks rather than validators of physical plau-
sibility. This motivates detection signals grounded in invariants
of image formation, which a generator would have to repro-
duce by implicitly solving the underlying inverse problems.
The sensor-noise residual is the canonical such invari-
ant. Photo-response non-uniformity and learned camera-model
fingerprints survive throughout a device’s lifetime and are
disrupted where two images are composited [3]. Noiseprint [3]
extracts a camera-model fingerprint disentangled from scene
content, and TruFor [2] builds an end-to-end forgery detector
around the Noiseprint++ fingerprint with a learned fusion
head. These methods are reported as effective in general
image-forgery settings, but the specific question of whether
the noise channel is complementary to an RGB appearance
backbone on face-swap manipulations—as opposed to redun-
dant with it, or destroyed by architectural choices—has not
been settled by a controlled ablation.
Terminology. We use complementary and redundant in
a precise, measurable sense throughout. Let A(·) denote
detection AUC for a given channel or fusion of channels.
Two channels are complementary when fusing them strictly
exceeds the better single channel beyond statistical noise,
A(fusion) > max{A(RGB), A(residual)},
i.e. the second channel supplies discriminative information the
first does not. They are redundant when fusion fails to exceed
the stronger single channel,
A(fusion) ≈max{A(RGB), A(residual)} ≤A(RGB),
i.e. the second channel adds nothing the first has not already
captured. Our question is whether the Noiseprint++ residual is
complementary to RGB in this sense for face-swap detection.
We address that question directly on FaceForensics++
(FF++) [1]. Our contributions are:
• A controlled three-model ablation (RGB-only, residual-
only, late-fusion) showing that an architecturally fused
Noiseprint++ residual channel does not improve over
RGB alone on FF++ (Wilcoxon p = 0.977), while the
residual branch alone is near chance.
• A seven-level bottleneck diagnostic that localizes the

II. RELATED WORK
Deepfake detection and generalization. FF++ [1] es-
tablished that convolutional detectors achieve strong in-
distribution accuracy but degrade sharply across manipula-
tion methods and datasets. The Self-Blended Images (SBI)
framework [4], building on Face X-Ray [5], generates training
fakes by self-blending a single source image, removing the
arXiv:2610.11683v1  [cs.LG]  8 Oct 2026


dependence on labelled fakes and yielding markedly better
cross-dataset transfer; we use an off-the-shelf SBI model as a
reference baseline.
Sensor-noise forensics. Noiseprint [3] learns a camera-
model fingerprint via a Siamese contrastive objective on same-
camera versus different-camera patches. TruFor [2] couples
the Noiseprint++ fingerprint with an RGB stream through a
learned fusion head and places an InstanceNorm layer at
the noise-branch input. Our study adopts that architectural
template as the basis for a face-swap-specific ablation and asks
where, in the extraction–processing–fusion pipeline, the signal
is or is not retained.
III. METHOD
A. Data and noise extraction
All experiments use face crops from FF++ [1] and its
DeepFakeDetection extension, partitioned at the video level so
that no source video contributes frames to more than one split
(19,571 train, 3,573 val, 4,675 test crops). Faces are detected
with MTCNN [8], the box expanded by 1.3× and square-
padded. Noise maps are precomputed with Noiseprint++ [2]
on the full frame at native resolution before cropping; this
extract-then-crop order preserves the camera-model fingerprint
at the face location, whereas running the extractor on the small
crop degrades its receptive-field context. Class imbalance is
handled with class-weighted cross-entropy. The full data pro-
tocol and training configuration are tabulated in the Appendix
(Table IV).
B. Three ablation models
• RGB-only: an ImageNet-pretrained Xception [6] back-
bone on the face crop with a three-layer head—isolating
the RGB appearance signal.
• Residual-only: a ResNet [7] backbone on the 299 × 299
Noiseprint++ map with the identical head; its first layer
is InstanceNorm2d, following the TruFor architec-
ture [2].
• Late-Fusion: parallel RGB and noise branches concate-
nated into a joint head—testing whether the noise signal
is complementary to RGB.
Models are trained with AdamW, a tri-rate learning-rate
schedule with three-epoch warmup, ReduceLROnPlateau
on validation macro-F1, gradient clipping, and a progressive
backbone-unfreezing schedule.
C. Seven-level bottleneck diagnostic
To localize the residual branch’s failure independently of the
deep network, we run a progressive probe on the precomputed
noise maps via five-fold cross-validation on 3,000 FF++ test
samples. The seven levels are: (L1) logistic regression on
mean |noise|; (L2) logistic regression on six scalar statis-
tics (mean, std, energy, max, kurtosis, pixel count); (L3) a
small MLP on those six features; (L4) logistic regression on
InstanceNorm-pooled noise; (L5) logistic regression on
raw-pooled noise; (L6) a two-layer ConvNet on raw noise;
(L7) the same ConvNet with InstanceNorm before the first

D. Context-crop control
We additionally test whether expanding the crop from 1.3×
to 2.7× the box—to capture the blending boundary where
PRNU discontinuities should be most visible—helps. The
same probe is applied to both crop scales on 2,000 samples
each from FF++ and Celeb-DF, alongside a classifier-free
corpus signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) comparing between-class
to within-class variance of the noise statistics.
E. Fixed-fusion variants
Finally, two variants (five seeds each) directly remove the
diagnosed bottleneck: StatNoise-Fusion bypasses spatial pro-
cessing and fuses thirteen scalar noise statistics with Xception
features through a small MLP; ResAware-Fusion replaces
the noise branch’s InstanceNorm with BatchNorm and
processes the noise crop at native resolution, preserving the
per-sample statistics through the spatial pipeline.
IV. RESULTS
A. Ablation and cross-dataset transfer
Table I reports video-level AUC (frame-level for DFF) in
distribution and across three further corpora [9]–[11]. RGB-
only and Late-Fusion are statistically indistinguishable on
FF++ (Wilcoxon paired test on per-video squared errors,
p = 0.977 after Bonferroni correction), the residual branch is
near chance everywhere, and the SBI reference—an orthogonal
training-data prior—transfers best across datasets. Adding the
noise branch buys nothing over RGB: RGB-Only and Late-
Fusion are indistinguishable at both frame and video level,
while Residual-Only stays near chance.
B. The bottleneck is per-sample InstanceNorm
Table II resolves where the residual signal is lost. The noise
maps do carry a discriminative signal, and it is statistical: an
MLP on six scalar statistics (L3) reaches AUC 0.747, well
above chance. But the same statistics after InstanceNorm
pooling (L4) collapse to 0.554.

Eq. (1) subtracts the per-sample mean µc and divides by the
per-sample standard deviation σc. It therefore discards exactly
the first- and second-moment information of each noise map—
the per-sample mean and the variance/energy—and rescales

dominate the discriminative six-feature set (mean, std, energy,

survive it in principle; the empirical L3→L4 collapse to near
chance shows that, once the first two moments are removed,
the residual carries little discriminative signal on its own.
The Residual-Only ConvNet, which applies InstanceNorm
before any deep convolution, therefore never sees the discrimi-
native statistics; L7 (ConvNet with InstanceNorm) recovers
only part of the ceiling. Fig. 1 visualizes the L3→L4 collapse.

D. Fixing the bottleneck does not recover complementarity
Table III reports the two fixed-fusion variants. Both deliver a
non-trivial noise contribution at the fusion point—confirming
the diagnosis—yet both underperform RGB-only and the orig-
inal Late-Fusion on every dataset. The original Late-Fusion
learned to ignore a near-chance noise gradient and tracked
RGB-only; the fixed variants receive a stronger but redundant
noise signal and are pulled down by the weaker modality. This
is the signature of redundancy, not complementarity.
E. Robustness corroborates redundancy
A genuinely complementary noise channel should make
Late-Fusion behave differently from RGB-Only under pertur-
bations that degrade texture and sensor noise at different rates.
To test this directly we compute, for each perturbation family,
the paired per-dataset difference ∆AUC = Late-Fusion −
RGB-Only at frame level across the three video datasets
(FF++, Celeb-DF, DFDC; DFF is excluded as image-only
and near chance for both models), pairing within each dataset
so that between-dataset scale differences cancel. Fig. 2 plots
∆AUC with 95% confidence intervals.
The fusion difference is statistically indistinguishable from
zero across the entire sweep. Pooled over all operating points
the mean ∆AUC is −0.003 (max |∆| = 0.035); it is never
significantly positive at any JPEG, blur, resize, or gamma
level, and the only point whose CI excludes zero is neg-
ative (Gaussian blur σ = 2: −0.017). Adding the noise
branch therefore never improves robustness and, where it
has any measurable effect, slightly degrades it—the paired-
perturbation counterpart of the in-distribution Wilcoxon result
(p = 0.977). For contrast, the same paired difference for the
SBI reference (SBI−RGB-Only) is positive at every operating
point (means +0.04 to +0.17, largest under heavy JPEG),
confirming that the comparison readily resolves a genuinely
different model. The noise-fusion curve, by contrast, sits on
the zero line—the most direct evidence that the fusion head


has learned to ignore the near-chance noise gradient rather
than exploit it.
V. DISCUSSION
The evidence supports a single, mechanistic account. (i)
The Noiseprint++ residual does carry a face-swap signal on
FF++, but it is statistical—differences in the per-sample first
and second moments (mean, variance/energy) of the residual—
consistent with the Noiseprint family’s premise that composit-
ing disrupts the per-region camera-model fingerprint [3]. (ii)
That signal is destroyed inside the trained branch by the per-
sample InstanceNorm layer that the TruFor template places
at the input [2]: as Eq. (1) shows, standardizing each sample
to zero mean and unit variance removes precisely the first
two moments that carry the signal. The collapse is invisible in
loss curves and is only exposed by the classifier-free probe.
(iii) Once the bottleneck is removed, the recovered signal is
redundant with what the RGB backbone already extracts from
the same blending operation: the fixed-fusion variants never
exceed RGB-only, and the original Late-Fusion tracks RGB-
only across the perturbation sweep (Fig. 2), consistent with the
fusion head learning to ignore the noise gradient. (iv) Cropping
geometry is not responsible—the tight crop is uniformly better.
The practical guidance follows directly. A noise-residual
side channel adopted from a general image-forgery detector
should not be assumed complementary for face-swap detec-
tion: if the discriminative signal is statistical, a per-sample
normalization at the branch input will erase it, and even when
preserved it may add nothing over RGB. The contrast with
the SBI reference is instructive: a physical prior injected at
the training-data level transfers across datasets far better than
this architectural side channel, suggesting that the locus of
injection matters more than the presence of the prior.
Limitations. The study is on the FF++ face-swap distribu-
tion; the conclusion is specific to that manipulation family and
to the Noiseprint++/TruFor template. We do not claim sensor-
noise residuals are useless for forgery detection in general—
only that they are redundant, not complementary, for face-swap
detection here, and that a common architectural choice silently
destroys the signal they do carry.
VI. CONCLUSION
We asked whether a learned camera-noise residual is
complementary to an RGB backbone for face-swap detec-
tion, and—if not—where the signal is lost. On FaceForen-
sics++, architectural fusion gives no improvement over RGB;
a seven-level probe localizes the failure to a per-sample
InstanceNorm layer that erases the statistical signal the
residual carries (AUC 0.747→0.554); a context-crop control
rules out cropping geometry; and two fixed-fusion variants
show that, once the signal is preserved, it is redundant with
RGB. The bottleneck probe is a reusable diagnostic for audit-
ing where an auxiliary forensic channel succeeds or fails.
