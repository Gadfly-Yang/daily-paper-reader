# A Data-Centric Framework for Detecting and Correcting Corrupted Labels 

Ha-Linh Nguyen, Hong-Anh Nguyen, Minh-Duc La, Thu-Trang Nguyen, Son Nguyen, and Hieu Dinh Vo ⋆

Faculty of Information Technology, VNU University of Engineering and Technology, Hanoi, Vietnam

Abstract. The performance of machine learning and deep learning mod-els largely depends on the quality of the training data. However, the quality of the real-world datasets is often compromised by noisy labels, which can substantially degrade model accuracy and reliability. To ad-dress this challenge, we propose Relabeler , an end-to-end data-centric framework for detecting and correcting corrupted labels. For corrupted label detection, Relabeler jointly leverages both local and global re-lationships among data instances to identify potentially noisy samples. After detecting suspicious instances, Relabeler further performs label correction by estimating the most probable clean label for each instance based on both its input features and observed noisy label. Extensive ex-periments across multiple datasets, noise types, and noise rates demon-strate that Relabeler consistently outperforms state-of-the-art base-lines, achieving up to 58% improvement in label correction precision and 6% improvement in downstream task performance.

## 1 Introduction 

Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL) have achieved impressive per-formance across various domains [17]. Central to the success of ML/DL models is the availability of high-quality labeled training datasets. However, constructing a large-scale and accurately labeled dataset is prohibitively expensive. In practice, labels are often collected via crowd-sourcing or automated annotation tools [21], both of which are inherently error-prone. Consequently, label noise , where data instances are assigned incorrect labels, is prevalent, with reported noise rates ranging from 8% to 38.5% in real-world datasets [23]. Such noisy labels can signif-icantly degrade model performance and lead to unreliable predictions [9,1,8,22]. Therefore, effectively handling label noise is essential for developing trustworthy ML/DL systems.

Existing approaches for handling label noise can generally be divided into two main categories: model-centric and data-centric . Model-centric approaches focus on improving the robustness of learning algorithms under noisy supervision. These methods typically employ techniques such as robust loss functions [19], regularization strategies [1], or meta-learning frameworks [10], which aim to pre-vent models from overfitting to incorrect labels. In contrast, data-centric ap-proaches seeks to improve the dataset quality by identifying and correcting cor-rupted labels either before or during the training process. Recent studies in this line of direction [8,9,26] use statistical patterns, structural assumptions, or ded-icated models to detect label errors and enhance data reliability. Compared with model-centric methods, data-centric approaches address the root cause of the problem by directly improving the quality of the data itself. The improvements are not limited to a specific model, but can benefit any down-stream learner trained on the refined dataset. This generality and reusability make data-centric approaches particularly practical for real-world applications. However, most existing studies [12,24,9,22] treat noisy label detection and cor-rection as separate tasks and mainly focus on detecting corrupted instances. This separation limits their ability to fully exploit the intrinsic relationship among in-put features, noisy labels, and clean labels in a unified framework. Furthermore, detecting suspicious instances alone is insufficient, as the remaining challenge is to accurately infer the correct labels for the identified noisy samples. To address these limitations, this paper proposes Relabeler , an end-to-end data-centric framework for handling label noise. Relabeler contains two integrated phases: corrupted label detection and corrupted label correction .In the corrupted label detection phase, Relabeler is motivated by the ob-servation that semantically similar instances are likely to share the same label. Building upon this intuition, Relabeler jointly leverages both local and global relationships among data instances to identify suspicious ones that are poten-tially mislabeled. Specifically, suspicious instances are identified as those that exhibit strong similarity while being associated with inconsistent labels. After detecting suspicious instances, Relabeler proceeds to the corrupted label correction phase, where it estimates the most probable clean label for each instance based on both its input features and its observed noisy label. In par-ticular, Relabeler models two complementary factors: how likely an instance belongs to a particular class based on its input features, and how labels are typ-ically corrupted in the dataset. By integrating these two sources of information through Bayesian inference, Relabeler infers the most probable clean label for each suspicious instance. To evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed framework, we conducted exper-iments on five widely used datasets covering image, text, and source code clas-sification tasks. Experimental results under various noise types and noise rates demonstrate that Relabeler consistently outperforms state-of-the-art meth-ods [27]. In particular, Relabeler achieves up to 58% improvement in label correction precision and 6% improvement in downstream task performance. ## 2 Problem Formulation 

Let X denote the feature space and Y = {1, . . . , C } be the label space. A clean dataset is defined as D = {(xi, y i)}ni=1 , where each instance (xi, y i) is indepen-dently drawn from the underlying distribution D = X × Y , and yi represents the correct label of xi. However, in real-world scenarios, we typically observe a noisy dataset eD = {(xi, eyi)}ni=1 , where each observed label eyi may differ from the corresponding clean label yi. An instance (xi, eyi) is considered corrupted/noisy if eyi̸ = yi, and clean otherwise. This paper formulates label noise handling as an end-to-end data-centric process consisting of two sequential phases: corrupted label detection and corrupted label correction .The goal of corrupted label detection is to identify potentially mislabeled instances. Given the noisy dataset eD, the detection phase partitions the data into two disjoint subsets eD = Dclean ∪ Dsuspicious , where Dclean contains instances predicted to have reliable labels, while Dsuspicious contains suspicious instances identified as potentially mislabeled. Subsequently, the corrupted label correction phase aims to repair labels of in-stances in Dsuspicious . Specifically, for each suspicious instance (xj , eyj ) ∈ Dsuspicious ,the objective of this phase is to infer a corrected label y∗

3.1 Corrupted Label Detection 

This phase aims to detect potentially mislabeled instances by identifying seman-tically similar instances that are associated with inconsistent labels. The under-lying intuition is that instances with similar features are likely to share the same label. Therefore, when similar instances are assigned different labels, some of them are likely to be mislabeled. To comprehensively verify labels, Relabeler 

examines data instances from both local and global similarity perspectives. First, Relabeler performs local verification by examining the labels of nearby instances in the feature space. Specifically, for instance xi, Relabeler 

verified whether its label eyi is consistent with those of its nearby neighbors. To achieve this, Relabeler employs the k-Nearest Neighbors ( k-NN) algorithm to retrieve the k nearest neighbors of xi. k-NN is adopted because it is widely used for local similarity analysis and has been shown to be relatively robust to label noise [4]. If the label of xi is consistent with the majority label among its neighbors, the instance is considered likely to be clean and is added to Dclean .Otherwise, it is regarded as suspicious and added to Dsuspicious .Although k-NN is effective in capturing local similarity, it only considers a limited neighborhood around each instance. Consequently, the local verification step can achieve high precision in identifying clean instances. However, it may incorrectly classify clean instances as suspicious, since an instance whose label differs from those of its nearby neighbors may still be clean when viewed from a broader global context. Such cases commonly arise near class boundaries or in regions with complex data distributions. To address this limitation, Relabeler 

performs an additional global verification step to further examine suspicious instances in a global similarity perspective. A Data-Centric Framework for Detecting and Correcting Corrupted Labels 5

For global verification , Relabeler uses Dclean to train a classification model Mclean . Since Dclean only contains likely clean instances, the resulting model is expected to provide reliable predictions. More importantly, Dclean can contain multiple local regions, enabling Mclean to capture broader and more general patterns across the entire dataset. For each instance xi ∈ Dsuspicious , if the label predicted by Mclean matches its original label, the label of xi is con-sistent with global patterns captured by Mclean . In this case, xi is considered to be likely clean and is moved to Dclean . Otherwise, the instance remains in 

Dsuspicious as a potentially corrupted sample. This process is performed itera-tively. After each iteration, newly verified clean instances are added to Dclean ,enabling the classification model to be progressively refined using increasingly reliable training data. The iterative process continues until no additional clean instances can be identified. 

In summary , the overview of the corrupted label detection phase is shown in the top half of Fig. 1. This phase takes the noisy dataset eD as input and performs two verification steps. First, Relabeler conducts local verification to identify likely clean samples whose labels are consistent with those of their similar instance in the local context. Next, Relabeler performs global verification to further identify likely clean samples whose labels are consistent with broader global patterns across the dataset. The output of this phase is a partition of eD

into two subsets: a clean subset Dclean and a suspicious subset Dsuspicious .

First, to model the distribution P (y | x), Relabeler trains a clean label predictor Mclean using the subset Dclean . Since Dclean contains instances whose labels are likely correct, it enables Mclean to learn the underlying relationship between the input features and clean labels. Second, to approximate the noise transition probability P (˜ y | x, y ), Relabeler trains a noisy label estimator 

Mnoisy . This model takes both instance x and a candidate label y as input, and predicts the likelihood of observing the noisy label ˜y.Training Mnoisy requires transition data in the form of triples (( x, y ), ˜y) that explicitly characterize how clean labels are corrupted into noisy ones. However, such transition data is not directly available. To address this issue, we construct a transition dataset Dtrans = {(( xi, y i), ˜yi) | (xi, y i) ∈ Dclean } by injecting synthetic noise to Dclean in a realistic manner. To generate realistic noisy labels, Relabeler trains a separate neural net-work called noise simulator Msimulator using the suspicious subset Dsuspicious .Since Dsuspicious contains instances that are likely to be corrupted, training on this subset enables Msimulator to learn the statistical relationship between input features and the noisy labeling patterns present in the dataset. After training, 

Msimulator is applied to instances in Dclean to generate synthetic noisy labels. Specifically, for each instance (xi, y i) ∈ Dclean , the simulator predicts a noisy la-bel ˜yi = Msimulator (xi), forming a triplet (xi, y i, ˜yi). These triplets are then used to construct the transition dataset Dtrans for training the noisy label estimator. Using the transition dataset Dtrans , we train Mnoisy to learn the noise tran-sition function. By training Mnoisy on Dtrans , the model learns to capture how a clean label y might be mislabeled to ˜y given the feature x. For each input feature x, Mnoisy is designed to output a C × C transition matrix Tx, where C

is the number of classes in the label space Y. Each element Tx[l, k ] represents the conditional probability P (˜ y = k | x, y = l). This is the likelihood of observing a noisy label ˜y = k given input x and its candidate clean label y = l.The workflow of the corrupted label correction phase is illustrated in the bot-tom half of Fig. 1. First, the clean label predictor Mclean is trained on Dclean .In Relabeler , Mclean can reuse the classification model obtained from the corrupted label detection phase. Next, Relabeler trains the noise simulator 

Msimulator on Dsuspicious for simulating noisy labels and constructing the tran-sition dataset Dtrans . Using Dtrans , Relabeler subsequently trains the noisy label estimator Mnoisy . Finally, given a suspicious instance (x, ˜y), Relabeler 

employs Mclean and Mnoisy to compute P (y | x) and P (˜ y | x, y ) for each candi-date label y ∈ Y . These estimates are integrated using the formulation defined in Eq. 2, the repaired label y∗ is the label with the highest posterior probability. Although the label with the highest probability P (y | x) predicted by Mclean 

could be directly used to repair the label of x, relying on it alone is insufficient and potentially unreliable. While Mclean can capture the general relationship between features and clean labels, it does not account for the noise distribution in the dataset. By integrating predictions from both Mclean and Mnoisy , our method incorporates both the semantic alignment of features with clean labels and the statistical patterns of label corruption. Precision measures the accuracy of corrections, defined as the proportion of correctly changed labels among all changed labels; higher values indicate better performance. Error Rate quantifies the proportion of incorrect labels remaining in the dataset after the correction process; lower values indicate better performance.

## 5 Related Work 

Automated data annotation: Data annotation is essential yet costly for train-ing high-quality ML/DL models [16], which has motivated extensive research on automated and semi-automated labeling techniques [6,20,25,3]. While these ap-proaches can substantially reduce annotation effort, prior studies have reported limitations in the accuracy and consistency of generated labels [15,7]. 

Corrupted label detection: Numerous approaches [26,12,24,9,22,18] have been proposed to identify corrupted labels. For example, SENT [22] leverages a small trusted subset to model noise distribution and uses it to distinguish cor-rupted labels. Retrieval-based approaches [12] identify noisy labels by comparing an instance with its nearest neighbors. Cola [9] further improves detection by modeling both local and global instance relationships. While effective at identi-fying suspicious labels, these methods focus primarily on detection and typically rely on discarding or reweighting noisy instances. Relabeler is complementary to these approaches, as it operates on detected corrupted labels and explicitly repairs them rather than removing them from the dataset. 

Corrupted label correction: Only a limited number of studies [26,27] have explicitly investigated corrupted label correction from a data-centric perspective. 

Docta [26,27] detects unreliable annotations based on neighborhood disagree-ment and subsequently corrects them using heuristic strategies such as majority voting or ranking-based label replacement. In contrast, the majority of existing work adopts a model-centric perspective, focusing on designing learning algo-rithms that are inherently robust to noisy labels rather than explicitly repairing them [1,10,5,11]. Different from these approaches, Relabeler directly targets the label repair problem by explicitly modeling the relationship between clean labels, observed noisy labels, and input features. By formulating label correction 12 Nguyen et al. 

as a Bayesian inference problem, Relabeler infers the most probable clean la-bel for each instance, enabling systematic and principled correction of corrupted annotations. As a result, Relabeler complements model-centric noise-robust learning methods and provides a reusable, high-quality dataset that benefits subsequent learning tasks. 

## 6 Conclusion 

Label noise remains a critical challenge in building reliable ML/DL systems, as corrupted annotations can substantially degrade both model performance and robustness. Although recent data-centric methods have improved the detection of noisy labels, effective and reliable label correction methods remain relatively underexplored. To address this gap, we introduce Relabeler , an end-to-end data-centric framework for corrupted label detection and correction. Relabeler 

jointly leverages local and global relationships among data instances to identify suspicious samples, and further repairs corrupted labels by estimating the most probable clean label based on both input features and observed noisy labels. Ex-tensive experiments across diverse datasets, noise types, and noise rates demon-strate that Relabeler consistently improves label correction quality and en-hances downstream model performance, while also complementing existing noise detection approaches.
